\section{Related work and positioning}
\label{sec:related}

Transfer-matrix enumeration of meanders was introduced by Jensen
\cite{Jensen2000}, whose algorithm already uses planar connectivity states,
four local updates per crossing, weighted state merging, terminal-only
closure, and pruning by remaining crossings; the \LOW\ branch is a
height-restricted instance of this method. Di Francesco, Guitter and Jacobsen
\cite{DFGJ2000} develop a chronological transfer whose connectivity states
are organized by ballot numbers, with a stage-dependent inventory of states,
an explicit ordering, remaining-bridge constraints that prune states which
cannot be completed within the remaining crossings, and an estimate of the
inventory's post-midpoint peak and overall growth. The actual distinction
from the present work is elsewhere: their height counts processed arches
across the slit in a connectivity representation, whereas the partition here
is by the original combined source grade $k_j$; and they do not give the
first-hit complementary construction of \cref{sec:partition,sec:carrier} or
a proved $\Ostar(2^{n})$ bound. Jensen's recent talk \cite{Jensen2025} discusses future-connectivity
pruning and midpoint statistics and again reports signature growth near
$2.5^{n}$ as a fitted observation, not a proved bound. Planar link patterns and their local composition are classical
\cite{DFGG1997TL}; stack-based path joining and remaining-endpoint pruning
appear in exhaustive generation \cite{BobierSawada2010,SawadaLi2012}; Jacobsen
and Zinn-Justin \cite{JacobsenZinnJustin2002} encode knot-diagram slices by
left connectivity, right enclosing arches and an active line. Open meanders
without a persistent free-end coordinate have software precedent
\cite{Howroyd2015}. The identities \eqref{eq:odd} and \eqref{eq:even} are
explicit in La Croix \cite{LaCroix2003}. Determinantal approaches \cite{DiFrancesco1998} and structural
decompositions \cite{Belousov2026,CoriHetyei2024} give exact expressions or
factorizations but no evaluator with a proved binary bound. Alepis
\cite{Alepis2026} (version~2) gives, besides an explicit Catalan
face-factorized formula, an exact compact frontier transfer for closed
meanders, an analysis of its reachable state classes, and a proof that the
depth-pruned state class grows like $\varphi^{2}\approx2.618$ to the power
of the order; that work itself distinguishes this state-cardinality result
from a complete bit-complexity theorem. What the present paper adds relative
to it is the first-hit partition, the budget-forced early joins, and the
uniform all-stage complementary bound $\Ostar(4^{n-K})$ for the \HIGH\
branch, which together give the binary bound.

Relative to this literature, the cut bijection, the forced-prefix inequality and
the exterior identity are elementary consequences of stack discipline and
nesting, and the first-moment observer is ordinary generating-function algebra.
What we have not found elsewhere is the package: a disjoint partition by the
first crossing of the combined original height, an asymmetric schedule whose
budgets make the emitted prefix exact, early joins that keep the retained
boundary within $2(n-K)+O(1)$ ports at every stage, and a key of polynomially
many counters plus one noncrossing pairing that preserves labelled successor
multiplicities. Together these give the constructible complementary bound
$\Ostar(4^{n-K})$, and with the \LOW\ complement the $\Ostar(2^{n})$ theorem. We
know of no earlier unrestricted exact closed-counting algorithm with this bound;
this is an assessment of the literature we inspected, not a claim of worldwide
priority, and it does not concern practical speed. Polynomial-time evaluation
remains open.
